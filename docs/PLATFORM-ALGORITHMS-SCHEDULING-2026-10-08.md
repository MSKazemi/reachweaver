# Social platform algorithms and scheduling — evidence snapshot (2026-10-08)

**Status:** Researched, NOT proof of a connected account, live publishing capability, guaranteed reach, or a functioning best-time optimizer. Policies and interfaces may change. Recheck provider documentation when using or implementing.

**Goal:** Let a person produce truthful content in batches and arrange approved, account-specific posts for future delivery when their intended audience is likely to be active. Treat *native app scheduling*, *third-party API access* and *ReachWeaver execution* as **three different capabilities**. No platform provides a public universal formula for guaranteed reach.

## Confidence and evidence taxonomy

- **Official**: provider documentation, engineering description, or current product help. An official high-level factor is not a complete model or permanent ranking weight.
- **Observed**: aggregate timing association in an observational analytics dataset. Not proof that posting at that time causes incremental reach. Do not use as a guarantee or a substitute for an account's audience insights.
- **Hypothesis**: operational recommendation or sensible creative principle to test per audience and format. Mark it as such.

**Time convention:** Timing suggestions below are *audience-local*, not necessarily the operator's local time. Never schedule with a bare clock hour; keep IANA audience time zone, explicit date/offset and UTC, paying attention to DST. If the audience is split across Europe and the US, create separate target cohorts or alternate windows. Do not silently assume Europe/Rome is also the reader's timezone.

**Caveat:** Existing ReachWeaver “schedule” means *local outbox holds a UTC due timestamp*; `publish` is a dry run until both explicit live flag and authorization. No user social account has been connected or real-world tested. LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, Threads, X, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest and Reddit are **not** currently published by ReachWeaver.

## Best-time selection policy

See the **standalone Claude skill** `.claude/skills/reachweaver/references/timing-intelligence.md` for an auditable selection procedure. Apply in this order:

1. **No authorization -> no scheduling/publishing.** Drafting can be offline. Actual platform-native scheduling or API publishing needs the human's verified account and exact-content approval.
2. **Use first-party per-account active-viewer insights** when available (e.g. YouTube Audience). For every channel, distinguish audience geography from account owner's timezone. Recheck format/account differences.
3. **Use observed 2026 industry windows only as low-confidence warm-starts** if insufficient own data. The Buffer study is based on Buffer customers, aggregated posts, and non-experimental differences. The complete latest public study is [Buffer 52M+ posts](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/); don't present a time as empirically optimized for this specific brand.
4. **Propose multiple slots**, each with audience-local date, IANA timezone, UTC, evidence source/checked date, format, reasoning, confidence, schedule transport and owner approval status. Separate exploratory slots from higher-priority audience activity slots.
5. **Respect operational rules**: no prohibited automation, API capabilities, account post limits, unscheduled community posts or cross-brand mixing; honor holidays, sensitive announcements, review timelines, content embargoes, practical reply coverage, min post spacing and launch-day dependencies.
6. **Measure at matched ages**: 24h, 7d and optionally 28d; collect platform-native denominator and qualified outcomes (profile visits, website sessions, stars/downloads) where accessible. Missing values are unknown. Look for persistent differences across reasonably similar content, not one viral outlier.
7. **Learn conservatively**: update proposed slots after enough *comparable* account history, keep exploration to avoid optimizing solely around a noisy initial peak, log competing explanations and report uncertainty. Do not describe observational changes as proven causal improvement.

## Practical 2-week batching model

Create 10 approved, platform-specific drafts at a convenient time; let the skill propose local-time target slots and a manual handoff for native schedulers. Use one target audience and one experiment per brand. For LinkedIn, schedule in its native UI; for YouTube and TikTok, complete the video upload through approved native interfaces; for Bluesky/Mastodon/Telegram use the already implemented text adapter **only after** credentials, user-approved test send, and a reliable persistent local worker are validated. Unsupported accounts stay drafts with a reminder/manual-export checklist, not phantom scheduled posts.

Do not use fake engagement, auto-generated comments, artificial cross-account coordination, repeated near-identical mass posts, scraping login sessions, or API restriction circumvention.

## Research maintenance and technical gaps

- Last researched **2026-10-08**; validate platform limits and APIs again at execution time and at least monthly. Source dates differ; do not conflate old blog announcements with tested 2026 UI.
- Algorithmic weights are not generally published; the notable exception of X public code still does not expose every serving factor and does not authorize manipulating recommendations.
- ReachWeaver now has a small offline `calendar-plan` CLI with transparent, conservative candidate windows, IANA timezone/DST validation, blackout and spacing checks and optional approved-text-only local queueing. It **does not** learn the optimal time from native analytics or guarantee improvement. Per-account heatmaps, automated analytics collection, a durable cloud worker, asset/media upload and official API integration for most networks are still future work; see [calendar quickstart](CALENDAR-PLANNER.md).
