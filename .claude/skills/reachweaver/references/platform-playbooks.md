# Official-source social-media growth playbooks, October 2026

Load this for platform-native production, hooks, SEO/discovery, timing, video and organic engagement. **This is a curated operational synthesis, not a secret formula for proprietary recommendation engines.** Every network can change ranking, specs and API permissions. Consult official sources at campaign time and mark non-official tips as *hypotheses to test*. Do not copy others' exact skill instructions or automate prohibited participation.

## Common rules for every network

- **Source before story:** show a real release, runnable example, measured result, first-party photo or documented limitation. A strong opening must be repaid immediately with proof.
- **One pillar asset, multiple genuinely new treatments:** produce an original demonstration or article, extract evidence-supported moments, then reframe for each audience. Avoid verbatim duplicate floods or third-party clip reuploads.
- **Audience-native design:** first seconds/line express the viewer's problem and concrete payoff; demonstrate rather than advertise; no false numeric superlatives, fake scarcity, purchased social proof or invented endorsements.
- **Search discovery:** use words your audience searches for in truthful title, visible caption, spoken explanation and accessible description; useful keywords outperform irrelevant hashtag stuffing. Validate current hashtag, length, music and licensing requirements per network.
- **Accessible production:** accurate subtitles, meaningful alt text, legible screenshots, clear audio, contrast and understandable context without sound. Show rights/licensing for screenshots, footage and music. Never label a storyboard as a rendered asset.
- **Consent + reputation:** manual review of claim, exact identity, account, moderation norms and URL before publishing; no auto-liking, network-of-accounts amplification, mass unsolicited replies or subverted rate limits.
- **Experiment not superstition:** varying hook, first-frame proof, duration, format, thumbnail or time are **hypotheses**, not guarantees. Predefine metric, observation window and stop rule, and beware selection bias.

## LinkedIn: useful professional insight, not empty thought leadership

**Documented:** LinkedIn's [Page guidance](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/linkedin-pages/best-practices) recommends complete company profiles, regular posts, custom visuals, video, thoughtful discussions and analytics. It reports **correlational Page** performance examples (e.g., images more comments; video more engagement); those are **not guarantees for an individual author**.

**Do:**
1. Name the exact professional problem in the opening; show one design decision, diagram, concise demo, validated benchmark or surprising *true* failure.
2. Explain trade-offs and a replicable lesson. State what evidence supports, and disclose project affiliation. Avoid formulaic inspirational filler.
3. Choose a plain-text lesson, real screenshots, short native video, or an authored PDF/document when a system architecture needs sequence.
4. Add one contextual CTA (reproduce the example, discuss a limitation, try documented open source); respond to legitimate technical questions.
5. Test personal versus Page accounts only with separately authorized permissions; compare meaningful conversations and technical clicks, not just impressions.

**Avoid:** blanket statements that "external links are punished" or "commenting in the first hour guarantees growth". Test actual outcomes; do not move links into comments solely because an unaudited skill recommends it.

## YouTube long-form + Shorts: discoverable, retained demonstrations

**Documented:** YouTube describes recommendations as personalized using **clicks, watch time, viewer satisfaction, shares and other signals**, not one fixed formula: [recommendations](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/). Its [creator metrics guide](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/master-these-4-metrics/) highlights CTR, retention and traffic sources.

1. Start an engineering demo with the observable outcome (screen recording), then specify the problem and system; do not spend the opening on logos or generic intros.
2. For searchable long-form, use a title that states the actual task and tool, a thumbnail that makes an accurate promise, descriptive chapters where appropriate, technical prerequisites and links to reproducible source.
3. For Shorts, use one tangible idea / result, visually understandable in the first moments, with correct subtitles and an intelligible standalone explanation.
4. Inspect audience-retention dips, traffic source (Search / Suggested / Browse / External), *engaged views* and useful downstream visits. YouTube changed its basic **views counting from August 24, 2026**; preserve definition and period: [Analytics help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281).
5. For **eligible public long-form videos**, YouTube Studio [Test & compare](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714) can test **up to three thumbnails** concurrently and evaluates **watch-time share**, not CTR alone. Do not present sequential title edits as randomized A/B tests. Not available for Shorts in cited eligibility guidance.

## Instagram Reels / carousels: original visual teaching

**Documented:** Meta's [Instagram creator Best Practices](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/) offers personalized creation, engagement, reach and guideline advice in Professional Dashboard; [Meta's Jan 2026 update](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/01/2026-ai-drives-performance/) says **75% of US Instagram recommendations** in Q4 2025 came from original posts. This is platform-wide, **not an individual account target**.

1. Film/produce original screen demos, comparison visuals or visual explainers. Repurpose a *topic*, not lightly edited copies of a third party's media.
2. Open with a visible result and concise on-screen problem; make a self-explanatory Reel or carousel (question -> challenge -> real proof -> applicable takeaway).
3. Caption accurately, use legible overlays, meaningful relevant language/keywords and proper credits. Select an honest profile/website CTA rather than generic comment bait.
4. Use the account's professional dashboard and native insights to inspect reach, shares, saves and retention when actually available.
5. Respect original-content and recommendation-eligibility policies. Meta also describes [Facebook original-content prioritization](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/).

## TikTok: interest-led discovery, not magic hashtag combinations

**Documented:** [TikTok's recommender explanation](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/how-tiktok-recommends-content) identifies interactions (including watch-through versus skip), content information and user factors; relative weights change. Watch behavior often matters more than fixed posting-time recipes.

1. Translate the technical achievement into an immediate visual before/after or one understandable lesson for the intended audience.
2. Make the first visual frame meaningful without knowing the creator or product; use native captions and an accurate searchable topic description.
3. Observe real retention, shares, qualified replies and profile actions; isolate one variable in the next video.
4. Never scrape copyrighted trending audio or assume popularity of an unrelated trend produces relevant technical users.
5. TikTok's [Content Posting API](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/content-posting-api-get-started) can limit unaudited apps to private visibility; for ReachWeaver prepare **manual creator-ready media/script** until app approval and permission have actually been verified.

## Reddit: community-first help, not a scheduled promotion feed

**Documented:** [Reddit rules](https://redditinc.com/policies/reddit-rules) require authentic participation, compliance with each community's rules and avoidance of spam/content manipulation.

1. Find a relevant **question that actually needs an answer**; read the subreddit's exact self-promotion and flair policy before considering a submission.
2. Lead with reproducible diagnosis, transparent limitations and a standalone useful answer. Disclose when a referenced tool is yours.
3. Use a link only when permitted and directly relevant; invite technical criticism rather than ask for votes or mass share to communities.
4. Let the human author review and publish; respond as an actual participant. No voting automation, bulk Reddit posting, unsolicited DMs or fabricated testimonials.
5. Evaluate genuinely constructive replies and qualified adopters, not karma loops.

## Hacker News: manual original writing and occasional real launch

**Documented, strict:** [HN guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html) say **do not automate posting or submit generated / AI-edited text**. The [Show HN rules](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html) require a project people can actually try, with real technical substance and no vote solicitation. [Show HN access notice](https://news.ycombinator.com/showlim) currently warns of temporary restrictions for unfamiliar submitters.

**ReachWeaver permitted action:** do *private research*, verify prerequisites, prepare factual evidence inventory/checklist and remind the owner of guidelines. The human must independently compose the HN title and submission (without AI-generated or AI-edited text) and manually post if eligible. Do not export generated copy labeled "HN ready to paste." Major launch only, not every minor release.

## X, Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon: authentic network conversations

- **X:** concise source-backed stand-alone post or genuinely useful technical thread; publish through approved API or manual owner action. [X Rules](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-rules) prohibit artificial amplification, account manipulation and credential misuse. Do not assume an exact best time or limit without live validation.
- **Threads:** conversational topic, relevant community and constructive dialogue; try original thought plus context, not corporate press copy. Meta reported [500 million MAU](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/06/meta-launching-new-features-500-million-monthly-threads-users/) in June 2026; this does not promise an individual's impressions.
- **Bluesky:** technical observation with source, deliberate accessibility and native discussion; use official [post/facets API](https://docs.bsky.app/docs/tutorials/creating-a-post) when authorized. Text-only adapter in ReachWeaver remains live-account **unverified**.
- **Mastodon:** respect per-instance policy and community norms, add useful context and alt text; use official [status API](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/methods/statuses/) only when explicitly authorized. No synthetic engagement or mass mentions.

## Facebook, Pinterest, Snapchat and opt-in groups

- **Facebook:** test original Reels or relevant moderated Page/Group lessons where niche users already participate; Meta says unoriginal reuploads may be deprioritized: [official March 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/). Not a default first channel for deep enterprise platform engineering.
- **Pinterest:** for visual how-to diagrams, workflow visuals and keyword-led evergreen discovery. Only relevant if honest vertical graphics solve an audience query; avoid repeated identical pin floods.
- **Snapchat:** potentially large top-of-funnel audience but often high production cost and less direct conversion for technical B2B topics; test only with a deliberate user persona and original mobile-native video.
- **Telegram, Discord, WhatsApp, newsletters:** send valuable opt-in release notes, respond to actual questions and build retention. Group member counts are not cold discovery; never mass-invite or auto-DM.

## Community and conversion adjuncts (not "mass social" networks)

- **DEV Community:** original, tested how-to and source code links; [tag-specific rules](https://dev.to/t/socialmedia) permit some self-promotion but ask for restraint.
- **GitHub:** make README, releases, examples, issue templates and onboarding actually useful; social discovery cannot rescue a broken first-use experience.
- **Product Hunt:** launch only when the product fits the audience and current guidelines; human-led engagement, not manufactured comments.
- **Search/SEO:** the owned documentation site and discoverable tutorial are durable assets; route social visits through transparent UTM links and first-party consented analytics, using MSKazemi/seo for deeper SEO where available.

## Reusable "attention test card" for Claude

For each platform variant output:
- **Proof:** exact original source, claim, timestamp, uncertainty
- **Audience/job:** named person who benefits, query/problem in their language
- **Format:** demonstrable asset, truthful 1-line/first-frame payoff, native pacing, rights and accessibility
- **Discovery:** literal natural search terms, platform/community rules and real approved account
- **Action:** one useful CTA and correctly tagged destination if possible
- **Evidence of success:** native metric with denominator/definition, qualified engagement, 24h/7d observation
- **Status:** factual documented practice vs creator hypothesis, direct publishing vs manual only
- **Iteration:** single next change if actual results justify it

**Anti-patterns:** blanket "best 9 AM Tuesday" rules; compulsory 3–5 hashtags; link-in-first-comment myths; fake urgency; generic AI inspirational posts; too many posts; auto-replies; mass subreddit cross-posting; assumed API permission; engagement pods; copying competitor media; marketing claims without reproducible evidence.
